**How the picture looks**

- **K13. Cast shadows land on open sky.** On a photo with objects in front of open sky, with **Cast shadows** on and the light at the side, each object throws its shadow onto the sky: a soft dark band on the sky beside the object, on the side away from the light. On a daylight photo of your own it is plain to see, not faint. A tester saw it on a photo of machines at a show: a dark smear on the sky beside the cab of a loader (issue 6). Since the rim fix (K48) nothing hides it, and on an outdoor photo it is the first thing you see: the independent check of Oct 11 saw a grey patch of sky beside a man's head and camera, and a smoky copy of each pole beside it. On the Rocket sample or `04-dark-rocket.jpg` it is a blurred darker column on the sky to the right of each tower; the one on the right can have a fairly definite top edge. Sky is too far away for anything in the picture to shade it, so this is wrong, and it is known. What helps: switch **Cast shadows** off, or raise **Height**, which shortens the shadows. Not being changed before the deadline; K14 says why. (The sky as a whole also gets darker away from the light. That is meant: the light is a lamp close to the picture, not the sun. **Ambient** lifts it.) Cases: S-07, T-04, Q-06, Q-12, H-12.

**The test of the graphics chip before your own photo (new in the depth check update of Oct 11)**

These are limits of the fix for issue 4, not faults you can see on a healthy device. Two of them ask for a report all the same; they say so.

- **K66. The test's limit rests on three healthy graphics chips.** A chip may work out your photo when its answer to the test picture is at most 6 times as rough as the processor's. Three healthy chips have been measured: the developer's Apple one, 3.27 on every run; a tester's Intel one (a Dell XPS 15, "intel gen-12lp"), 3.28 (issue 2, Oct 11); and the same laptop's NVIDIA one ("nvidia ampere"), 3.26 (issue 7, Oct 11). No AMD or healthy phone chip has been measured. Two faulty chips have met the test: a Qualcomm Adreno under Windows scored 185.52 and was turned away, and a Galaxy S21 Ultra's chip was turned away too (its score cannot be read on a phone). The limit cannot simply be raised: a grid of dimples that is only just visible on a plain wall already scores about 7. Your chip's score is wanted: P-14. Cases: P-14, P-15.
- **K67. A healthy graphics chip of another make could be sent to the processor.** If a working chip scores above 6, the depth line reads "depth: this browser, processor (the graphics chip failed a check)" although nothing is wrong with the chip. The picture is right, only slower: on the developer's Mac the processor takes about 4 to 5 seconds for the first photo after a reload and about 2 seconds for each later one, against about 0.9 and 0.2 seconds on the graphics chip. Not measured on a phone. **Do report this one**, with the yellow console line: that line holds the chip's scores, and it is the only way the limit gets corrected. Case: P-14.
- **K68. The test adds a short wait, once each time the page is loaded.** The test itself runs for about 0.15 to 0.25 seconds on the developer's Mac, before the first photo, while the label reads "Starting the depth model…", and only where the graphics chip is used. Measured from choosing the photo, the first photo came about 0.1 second later than before the update. Later photos on the same page are not tested again. A slower chip will take longer; that was not measured. A chip that has not answered the test after 20 seconds is given up, and the processor takes over. Case: P-14.
- **K69. Only the test picture is tested, not your photo.** A chip that gets the test picture right and then spoils real photos would not be caught: the depth line would read "graphics chip (WebGPU)" and the photo would still have the dimples. The Snapdragon fault does show on the test picture: on the tester's laptop, with `?depth=check`, the test turned the chip away with agreement 0.6130 and roughness 185.52 (issue 4, Oct 11; one laptop, one browser). **Dimples on a photo are always worth a report.** Cases: P-14, P-15.

- **K71. The list of chips known to be bad is one chip family on one system.** It holds Qualcomm graphics chips under Windows, from one laptop's report (a Snapdragon X with Adreno X1-85 graphics).
