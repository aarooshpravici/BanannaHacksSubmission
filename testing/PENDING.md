**The test of the graphics chip before your own photo (new in the depth check update of Oct 11)**

These are limits of the fix for issue 4, not faults you can see on a healthy device. Two of them ask for a report all the same; they say so.

- **K66. The test's limit rests on one healthy graphics chip.** A chip may work out your photo when its answer to the test picture is at most 6 times as rough as the processor's. Only one healthy chip has been measured, an Apple one: 3.27, the same on every run. No Intel, NVIDIA, AMD or phone chip has been measured. The limit cannot simply be raised: a grid of dimples that is only just visible on a plain wall already scores about 7. Your chip's score is wanted: P-14. Cases: P-14, P-15.
- **K67. A healthy graphics chip of another make could be sent to the processor.** If a working chip scores above 6, the depth line reads "depth: this browser, processor (the graphics chip failed a check)" although nothing is wrong with the chip. The picture is right, only slower: on the developer's Mac the processor takes about 4 to 5 seconds for the first photo after a reload and about 2 seconds for each later one, against about 0.9 and 0.2 seconds on the graphics chip. Not measured on a phone. **Do report this one**, with the yellow console line: that line holds the chip's scores, and it is the only way the limit gets corrected. Case: P-14.
- **K68. The test adds a short wait, once each time the page is loaded.** The test itself runs for about 0.15 to 0.25 seconds on the developer's Mac, before the first photo, while the label reads "Starting the depth model…", and only where the graphics chip is used. Measured from choosing the photo, the first photo came about 0.1 second later than before the update. Later photos on the same page are not tested again. A slower chip will take longer; that was not measured. A chip that has not answered the test after 20 seconds is given up, and the processor takes over. Case: P-14.
- **K69. Only the test picture is tested, not your photo.** A chip that gets the test picture right and then spoils real photos would not be caught: the depth line would read "graphics chip (WebGPU)" and the photo would still have the dimples. On each photo the page catches only an answer that is not numbers at all, or one that is the same value everywhere. The Snapdragon fault is expected to show on the test picture too, but nobody has run the test on that chip (open item 52). **Dimples on a photo are always worth a report.** Cases: P-14, P-15.
- **K70. Broad, uneven noise is not caught.** The test looks for roughness at the scale of the grid the model works in, squares 14 pixels wide. Lumps several squares wide pass it: with such noise added by a program, 45 pixels wide and 3% of the depth's range high, the test still passed and a lit wall looked gently lumpy, with no grid. That is not the fault that was reported. Case: P-14.
- **K71. The list of chips known to be bad is one chip family on one system.** It holds Qualcomm graphics chips under Windows, from one laptop's report (a Snapdragon X with Adreno X1-85 graphics). On such a computer the chip is not tried at all, so a photo of your own always takes the processor's time, also if a later browser or Windows update puts the fault right. The page knows the chip by the maker's name the browser gives out ("qualcomm") and by the browser saying it runs on Windows. A browser that gives another name is not recognised, and the chip is then left to the test. Qualcomm chips in Android phones are not on the list: nobody has reported one, and they are left to the test too. Cases: P-15, H-12.
